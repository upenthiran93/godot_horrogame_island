# Backstory — DRAFT (v7, not final)

> Working draft. The user isn't happy with the ending of the owner's ritual yet, so Parts 5–9 are provisional. TBD = not decided. Inspired by Laukaste and Hora (user's reference), BioShock (a puppeteer who gives the orders), and The Shadow over Innsmouth (a cover-up and a bargain with something from the sea).

## The rule of the god
**The god's gifts only work when they are given willingly. Taken by force, they corrupt.** Every character below is judged by this rule.

**Corollary:** a human can't capture or bind the god. The colonist tried, and he became its servant. The god comes through only when it chooses to.

## Part 1: The festival (before the outsiders)
- A deity from another universe came to the island. It was impressed by the village and their peaceful way of life, and it became their friend.
- It cured their illnesses and gave them long life.
- Once a year, the village held a festival. They used **three crystals** to open the portal, so the god could visit and be worshipped. The god gave each villager a **healing crystal** in return, which they used to heal themselves and live longer.
- The **black stone** is the sign that the portal is thinning. It rises out of the water when the door is near.

## Part 2: The colonist (the first monster)
- A colonial ship wrecked near the island. Its captain and several crew were badly injured, and the village healed them.
- The captain saw the healing. He asked how it worked, and the villagers told him everything.
- He wanted that power for his own country. He joined the village and took part in the festival, pretending to be one of them.
- On the festival day, he turned on the villagers and slaughtered them. He took the three crystals, opened the circle without the god's consent, and tried to take the god's power for himself and his country.
- The god was wounded in the struggle and furious. It punished him by turning him into its servant, a phantom beast that roams the island. He is **the Shepherd** (name TBD: the colonist's name).
- The massacre wiped out the village entirely. Its culture disappeared with it. The festival never happened again.
- The three crystals were scattered. Some were lost in the ruins, and one remained where the village stood.

## Part 3: The quiet centuries
- The island was empty. Only ruins and the Shepherd remained.
- The myth survived only through the mainland fishing villages that had traded with the island before the massacre. The fishing captain in the story knows it that way.
- The black stone kept rising from time to time. Each time, the Shepherd was a little more violent.

## Part 4: The owner (the billionaire)
- He is a billionaire, the founder or chairman of one of the world's biggest companies.
- He became ill with an incurable disease and grew obsessed with healing and long life.
- Through research, he found the legend of the god and the island. He believed the god's gift was the cure he needed.
- He bought the island through his company and built a **private research center** on the mountain. It was a cover: officially it was for research, and in secret it was for digging up the old ritual site. A private center needs no military inspections.
- He hired **Elias** as his head of security and personal guard. Elias was ex-military, and the owner trusted him with the search.
- With Elias's security team, the company recovered the **three crystals** from the village ruins.
- He also hired scientists for the research. One of them is **the researcher** (name TBD).

## Part 5: The plan (provisional)
- **The owner's belief:** he can't capture the god, but the god heals whoever it chooses. A life offered willingly earns a gift. So he believes a life offered on the circle will earn him the cure.
- **The problem:** the owner can't make anyone offer willingly. So he decides to force the offering, using someone he chose, which is the "taken by force" crime the god punishes.
- **The chosen one:** the researcher. She is a scientist on his team who doesn't know what the circle is for. He plans to make her the offering.

## Part 6: Elias and the researcher (provisional)
- Elias falls in love with the researcher.
- He learns what the owner plans for her. He is still working for the owner, but he decides he won't let her be the offering.
- He tries to stop the ritual on the day.

## Part 7: The ritual and the blast (provisional)
1. The company team lights the circle with the three crystals. The researcher is placed in it.
2. The god's answer is not the owner's cure. It takes the researcher instead, and the void opens. It pulls in the teams around the circle.
3. The owner is pulled toward the void for forcing the offering. Elias is closest to the circle, and he pulls out the **crystal**, which breaks the pull on the owner. The owner is left half-tethered.
4. Elias's disruption unbalances the circle, and the chamber collapses in a blast. The void stays open. The company calls it an incident and abandons the island.
5. Elias is taken by the void with the others. The researcher is dead. He kept the crystal, or dropped it in the chamber. Either way, it's something the player has to recover.

## Part 8: The owner after the ritual (provisional)
- The god punished him, and he survived in a partial state. He is **bound to the island's electrical systems**, not a body.
- He can't physically leave. His real body is still dying, and he's decaying.
- He controls lights, gates, generators, radios, cameras, and recordings.
- **His goal:** finish the ritual and get his body back. He needs the player to reopen the gate.
- He is **turning into the Shepherd**. If he doesn't finish in time, he becomes the same monster the colonist is.

### What he can and can't do (puzzle rule)
- **He can:** switch power on and off, open and close powered gates, drive the generator, switch lights, use radios and intercoms, read and play recordings, and use cameras and records.
- **He can't:** enter codes or press keypads. Keypads and locks need a human to type, so the player does the input.
- **He can lie:** he can give the player false codes or wrong instructions. The player checks records and notes to find the truth.

## Part 9: The letter and the voicemail (provisional)
- **Before the ritual**, Elias wrote a letter to his sibling, the protagonist. It says he loves them, that he worked for the company, and that he's not sure he'll come back.
- The letter reached the protagonist as a real, physical letter.
- Later, a voicemail came from Elias's own number, asking them to come. It was the owner, using Elias's voice and the island's systems.

## Part 10: The lost souls
- **Elias** is a lost soul trapped on the island. He is a ghost.
- The researcher is a lost soul too, and so are the research and survey teams who died in the ritual.
- The intercom voice in Chapter 2 ("I'm at the upper station") is the **owner, using Elias's recordings**. It was a lie.

## Part 11: The brother
- The protagonist is Elias's sibling. The company's report said Elias died in an incident. They didn't believe it.
- The protagonist booked passage with a fishing captain who knows the myth from the mainland.

## Part 12: The crossing (where the story begins in the game)
- The captain warns them: he won't set foot on the sand, and if the stone rises while they're ashore, no boat will come.
- The owner hears the boat radio and the protagonist's phone as they approach. He sees the brother's arrival as his chance.
- The captain drops the protagonist on the beach and leaves. The stone is out past the point. The way back is already collapsing.

## Places in the game (suggested)
- **The village ruins:** a side area where the player finds one of the three crystals. The carved remnants of the culture are still there.
- **The research center and the chamber:** where the ritual happened and the circle is.
- **The sandbar:** where Elias went, and where the stone rises.

## Endings (proposed)
- **Complete the ritual and let the owner go.** He gets a body, and the god takes the sacrifice.
- **Stop it and keep him trapped.** He stays bound to the island, and the Shepherd keeps hunting.
- **Finish it and take the immortality yourself.** The god grants it, but only if you give yourself to it willingly, and the corruption is the price.
